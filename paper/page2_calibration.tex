\section*{2. Main findings}\reportanchor{sec:main-findings}{Main findings}
\claimheading{1}{Calibration can improve confidence accuracy in Qwen3.5-9B and GPT-OSS-120B on out-of-distribution domains in \SP{} and \DB{}, and on in-distribution domains in \SP{}.}
\par\smallskip\textbf{Main findings.}
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{1.1. Jaccard calibration improves confidence accuracy on out-of-distribution domains in \SP{} and \DB{}, with thinking on and off.}
\begin{itemize}
\item \underline{Confidence accuracy improves.} Adding calibration changes \armA{} to \armB{}. Expected calibration error (ECE) measures the gap between confidence and Jaccard correctness. Lower ECE is better. Each model, dataset and thinking mode passes the adjusted test ($p=0.0038$).
\item For example, with thinking on, Qwen3.5-9B's \SP{} ECE improves from .193 to .113. GPT-OSS-120B's improves from .356 to .285 (Table~\ref{tab:ood-calibration}). Tests average the out-of-distribution domains in Table~\ref{tab:evaluation-panel}. Figure~\ref{fig:calibration-example} shows an example from saved answers.
\item The improvement also holds on in-distribution domains in \SP{} with thinking on ($p=0.020$ per model; Table~\ref{tab:in-distribution-domain-calibration}).
\end{itemize}
\item \textbf{1.2. Binary calibration improves confidence accuracy on out-of-distribution domains in \SP{} and \DB{} with thinking on.}
\begin{itemize}
\item For Qwen3.5-9B, ECE improves by .047 on \SP{} and .026 on \DB{}. For GPT-OSS-120B, the improvements are .292 and .083 ($p=0.0038$ each).
\item With thinking off, binary results depend on the dataset. The \hyperref[sec:calibration-ablations]{Calibration ablation findings} give the accuracy trade-off.
\end{itemize}
\end{itemize}
